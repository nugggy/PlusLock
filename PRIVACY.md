# Privacy policy

Pimp my Phone ("the app") is made by nugggy, an individual developer. This policy explains what the app does with your information.

Last updated: 11 October 2026

## The short version

Pimp my Phone works on your phone and keeps what it learns on your phone. It has no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no crash reporting. The developer never receives your notifications, your messages, your location or anything else about you.

The app only connects to the internet for two things: checking for updates on GitHub, and, if you switch it on, fetching the weather for the Living sky wallpaper.

## What the app reads, and why

### Notifications

If you give the app notification access, it reads the notifications that arrive on your phone, including who they're from and what they say. It uses them to:

- show edge lighting and the preview bubble
- keep the notification vault, so you can search old notifications and see messages people unsend
- match your keyword alerts and the find my phone codeword
- read notifications aloud, if you turn that on
- build the morning digest, if you turn that on

The vault is stored in a private database on your phone. It keeps notifications for the number of days you choose (30 by default) and you can clear it at any time under Notification vault. Notifications can contain other people's messages and personal information, so treat the vault like your messages app.

### The accessibility service

If you turn on the accessibility service, the app uses it to:

- know which app is in front, so app profiles, app lock, time limits and pausing edge lighting can work
- draw edge lighting, the camera ring, charging effects, the camera island, the volume panel and other overlays on top of the screen
- perform actions you ask for, such as going back, taking a screenshot or locking the screen
- see volume button presses, but only while the volume panel or volume-button track skipping is switched on

The accessibility service never reads the text or images on your screen, never records what you type, and never sends anything off your phone.

### Location

Location is only used if you turn on Real weather and sunrise for the Living sky wallpaper, and only after you grant permission. About once an hour the app sends your approximate location, rounded to about 1 km, to Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com) to get the weather and sunrise and sunset times. Open-Meteo's own privacy policy applies to that request.

### Other information kept on your phone

- **Screen time:** how long you use each app, for the screen time dashboard and app time limits.
- **Battery log:** your battery level and temperature over the last two days, for the battery health chart.
- **Settings:** everything you set up, including names you add under People, your keywords and your find my phone codeword.

None of this leaves your phone unless you export it yourself.

### Bluetooth and other permissions

- **Nearby devices (Bluetooth):** so a routine can tell which Bluetooth device connected. The device name stays on your phone.
- **Do Not Disturb access, Modify system settings and ADB extras:** so routines, app profiles, find my phone and the status bar settings can change those settings for you.
- **List of installed apps:** so you can pick apps for profiles, the side panel and routines. The list stays on your phone.
- **Camera (torch only):** the app turns the torch on and off. It never takes photos or video.

## What goes over the internet

| What | Where it goes | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Update check and downloads | GitHub (api.github.com and github.com) | Every few hours if automatic updates are on, or when you tap Check for updates |
| Rounded location for weather | Open-Meteo (api.open-meteo.com) | About once an hour, only if real weather is on |

Like any website, GitHub and Open-Meteo can see your internet (IP) address when the app contacts them. No other information about you is sent, and nothing is sent to the developer.

## Backups

- **Back up and restore** in the app saves your settings to a file you choose. It includes names you've added, your keywords and your find my phone codeword, so keep the file somewhere private. It doesn't include the notification vault, screen time or battery history.
- **Android's own backup:** the app opts out, so nothing it stores is copied to your Google account or moved to a new phone automatically.

## Sharing

The developer doesn't sell, share or receive your information. The only third parties the app talks to are the two in the table above.

Some features use parts of Android that may involve Google or OnePlus services. For example, reading notifications aloud uses whichever text-to-speech engine your phone is set to, and that engine's own privacy terms apply.

## Your choices

- Each feature is off until you switch it on, and you can switch any of them off again.
- You can revoke notification access, the accessibility service, location and other permissions at any time in Android settings.
- You can clear the notification vault in the app.
- Uninstalling the app deletes everything it has stored on your phone.

## Children

The app isn't designed for children under 13 and doesn't knowingly handle children's information beyond what arrives in the phone's notifications.

## Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be in the app and at github.com/nugggy/PlusLock, with a new "Last updated" date. Big changes will be shown in the app before they take effect.

## Contact
